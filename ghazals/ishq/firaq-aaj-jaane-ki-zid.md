# आज जाने की ज़िद न करो (Aaj Jaane Ki Zid Na Karo)

**Poet:** Firaq Gorakhpuri (फ़िराक़ गोरखपुरी)  
**Theme:** Ishq (Love)  
**Era:** 20th Century (1896-1982)

---

## Original (Hindi/Urdu - Devanagari)

आज जाने की ज़िद न करो  
यूंहि पहलू में बैठे रहो

हाये मर जाएंगे, हम तो लुट जाएंगे  
ऐसी बातें किया न करो

तुम ही सोचो ज़रा क्यों न रोकें तुम्हें  
जान जाती है जब उठते हो

तुम्हें अपनी क़सम, जान-ए-जां  
बात इतनी मेरी मान लो

कुछ नहीं चाहिए ऐ मोहब्बत के सिवा  
कोई ग़म ज़िंदगानी से नहीं

बस इसी बात पर आज रुठे हुए हो  
अब तुम्हीं मुझको मनाने चलो

और क्या देखना बाक़ी है  
मुझे देखो ही हम को देखते रहो

आज जाने की ज़िद न करो

---

## Transliteration (Romanized)

Aaj jaane ki zid na karo  
Yunhi pehlu mein baithe raho

Haaye mar jayenge, hum to lut jayenge  
Aisi baatein kiya na karo

Tum hi socho zara kyun na roken tumhein  
Jaan jaati hai jab uthte ho

Tumhein apni qasam, jaan-e-jaan  
Baat itni meri maan lo

Kuch nahin chahiye ai mohabbat ke siwa  
Koi gham zindagaani se nahin

Bas isi baat par aaj roothe hue ho  
Ab tumhi mujhko manaane chalo

Aur kya dekhna baaqi hai  
Mujhe dekho hi hum ko dekhte raho

Aaj jaane ki zid na karo

---

## Spanish Translation (Traducción Española)

No insistas en irte hoy  
Quédate así sentada a mi lado

Ay, moriré, seré destruido  
No digas tales cosas

Piensa tú misma por qué no debería detenerte  
Mi vida se va cuando te levantas

Te lo juro, amor de mi vida  
Al menos escucha esto que te digo

No quiero nada excepto amor  
No tengo ninguna pena por la vida

Solo por esto estás enojada hoy  
Ahora ven tú a contentarme

¿Qué más queda por ver?  
Mírame, sigue mirándome

No insistas en irte hoy

---

## Context / Notes

This is one of Firaq Gorakhpuri's most beloved romantic poems, expressing the tender plea of a lover asking their beloved not to leave. The poem captures the vulnerability and earnestness of love, with the repeated refrain "Aaj jaane ki zid na karo" (Don't insist on leaving today) becoming iconic in Urdu poetry.

The ghazal is notable for its conversational tone and emotional directness. Unlike many classical ghazals that use elaborate metaphors, this poem speaks in simple, heartfelt language that makes it deeply relatable.

It has been popularized through numerous musical renditions, most famously by Farida Khanum, whose soulful interpretation made it a classic of ghazal singing.

## About the Poet

**Firaq Gorakhpuri** (1896-1982), born Raghupati Sahay, was a distinguished Urdu poet and critic. Despite being Hindu by birth, he became one of the finest Urdu poets of his time. He was awarded the Jnanpith Award in 1969, India's highest literary honor. His poetry is known for its romantic sensibility combined with philosophical depth, and his mastery of the Urdu language and classical forms.
