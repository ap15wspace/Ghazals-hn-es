# रंजिश ही सही (Ranjish Hi Sahi)

**Poet:** Ahmad Faraz (अहमद फ़राज़)  
**Theme:** Ishq (Love)  
**Era:** Contemporary (1931-2008)

---

## Original (Hindi/Urdu - Devanagari)

रंजिश ही सही दिल ही दुखाने के लिए आ  
आ फिर से मुझे छोड़ के जाने के लिए आ

पहले से मरासिम न सही फिर भी कभी तो  
रस्म-ओ-रह-ए-दुनिया ही निभाने के लिए आ

किस किस को बताएंगे जुदाई का सबब हम  
तू मुझसे ख़फ़ा है तो ज़माने के लिए आ

कुछ तो रब्त-ए-ख़ास था बहर हाल तेरे साथ  
तेहमत ही सही दुनिया मुंह में आने के लिए आ

कुछ और ज़माना भी मेरे साथ रहे तो  
अपनी जफाएं कुछ अदा करने के लिए आ

यूं ही नहीं दुनिया से ख़फा रहते हैं फ़राज़  
कुछ लोग भी हैं आशना रंजिदा होने के लिए

---

## Transliteration (Romanized)

Ranjish hi sahi dil hi dukhaane ke liye aa  
Aa phir se mujhe chhod ke jaane ke liye aa

Pehle se marasim na sahi phir bhi kabhi to  
Rasm-o-rah-e-duniya hi nibhaane ke liye aa

Kis kis ko bataayenge judaai ka sabab hum  
Tu mujhse khafa hai to zamaane ke liye aa

Kuch to rabt-e-khaas tha baharhaal tere saath  
Tehmat hi sahi duniya munh mein aane ke liye aa

Kuch aur zamaana bhi mere saath rahe to  
Apni jafaayen kuch ada karne ke liye aa

Yun hi nahin duniya se khafa rehte hain Faraz  
Kuch log bhi hain aashna ranjida hone ke liye

## Spanish Translation (Traducción Española)

Aunque sea con rencor, ven solo para herir mi corazón  
Ven de nuevo solo para abandonarme y partir

Aunque no haya la cercanía de antes, al menos a veces  
Ven solo para cumplir con las formalidades del mundo

¿A cuántos les contaremos la razón de nuestra separación?  
Si estás enojada conmigo, ven al menos por el bien del mundo

De todos modos había alguna conexión especial contigo  
Aunque sea una acusación, ven para que el mundo hable

Para que el mundo permanezca un poco más conmigo  
Ven a cometer algunas de tus crueldades

No es sin razón que Faraz permanece molesto con el mundo  
Algunas personas están destinadas a estar resentidas

## Context / Notes

"Ranjish Hi Sahi" is one of Ahmad Faraz's most beloved ghazals about love, longing, and the pain of separation. The opening couplet has become iconic in Urdu poetry, expressing the paradox of wanting to see one's beloved even if it means experiencing pain.

The ghazal captures the essence of unrequited or troubled love, where the lover would rather endure hurt than be completely separated from the beloved. Each couplet builds on this theme, showing how love makes one willing to accept even cruelty just to maintain some connection.

The poem has been sung by numerous artists including Mehdi Hassan, Ghulam Ali, and others, making it a staple of ghazal performances. The simplicity of language combined with profound emotional depth makes it accessible yet deeply moving.

---

## About the Poet

**Ahmad Faraz** (1931-2008) was one of Pakistan's most celebrated Urdu poets. Known for his romantic poetry and progressive political views, Faraz wrote extensively about love, separation, and social justice. His ghazals are characterized by their emotional intensity and accessibility. He received numerous awards including the Hilal-e-Imtiaz, Pakistan's second-highest civilian honor. Despite facing exile for his political views, he remained committed to poetry that spoke truth to power while also capturing the timeless themes of human emotion.
